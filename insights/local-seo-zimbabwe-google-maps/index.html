How to get your Zimbabwean business on Google Maps and rank locally

A step-by-step guide to getting your Zimbabwean business onto Google Maps: profile setup, verification, categories, reviews, listings and tracking.

7 November 2026 · 7 min read · By the Smart Click Media team

When someone in Harare searches for a plumber, a pharmacy or a hardware store, Google often shows a map and a short list of nearby businesses before any ordinary website. Getting onto that map is free. Showing up well on it takes care and steady work. This guide covers each step in the order we'd tackle it, from setting up your Google Business Profile to tracking the calls it brings in.

How Google decides which businesses to show

Google says local results depend on three things: relevance (how well your profile matches the search), distance (how far you are from the searcher, or from the place they named) and prominence (how well known and well reviewed you are, online and off). You can't change your distance from every customer, but most of the steps below improve relevance, prominence or both.

Step 1: Claim or create your Google Business Profile

First, search for your business on Google Maps. If a listing already exists, claim it rather than creating a duplicate, which would split your reviews and confuse customers.

  • Use a Google account owned by the business, not by a member of staff, and add a second owner or manager so you're never locked out.
  • Use your real business name, exactly as it appears on your signboard and invoices. Adding extra words, such as "Cheap Plumber Harare 24hrs", breaks Google's guidelines and can get a profile suspended.

Getting verified

Google decides which verification methods you're offered, and for many businesses it's now a short phone video. Film one continuous clip, with no editing, showing:

  • the street outside, nearby landmarks and your signage
  • the business at work: equipment, stock, branded vehicles or uniforms
  • proof that you run it, such as unlocking the door, opening the till or showing business documents

If it fails, follow Google's instructions, fix what was missing and try again, rather than opening a new profile.

Step 2: Choose your categories carefully

Your primary category is one of the most important choices you'll make. Pick the most specific option for your main business: "Hardware store" tells Google far more than "Store". Add secondary categories only for other things you genuinely do. The categories shown under competitors on the map are a useful guide. Then fill in the Services section, with a short description of each service and, where you can, a starting price.

Step 3: Shopfront or service area?

If customers come to you, show your address and check the map pin. Where street numbers are missing or unclear, drag the pin to your exact gate and put clear directions on your website's contact page.

If you travel to customers and don't serve them at your premises, as many plumbers, solar installers and home-based businesses do, hide your address and set a service area. List the towns or suburbs you really cover, for example Harare, Chitungwiza and Ruwa. Google's guidelines ask you to stay within about two hours' drive of your base, and profiles tend to show most strongly near their base. Only open a second profile where you have real, staffed premises. Reach other towns through location pages instead (see Step 8).

Step 4: Complete every section and add real photos

  • Phone: a number that's always answered. If it's on WhatsApp too, say so on your website.
  • Hours: your normal hours, plus special hours for public holidays such as Independence Day and Christmas.
  • Website: your homepage or, for a branch, that branch's own page.
  • Description: up to 750 characters on what you do, where and for whom, in plain language.
  • Photos: your logo, a cover photo, your premises from the street, the inside, your team, products and finished jobs. Use real photos, not stock images, and add new ones monthly.

Step 5: Post updates regularly

Google lets you publish short posts on your profile, such as updates, offers and events. A post every week or two, with a photo you took, shows people you're open and active. Ideas include new stock, a finished installation in Greendale, holiday trading hours or a seasonal offer.

Step 6: Earn reviews and reply to every one

Reviews affect both where you appear and whether people choose you. A steady flow of recent reviews is more convincing than an occasional burst.

  1. Copy the review link from your profile. It takes customers straight to the review form.
  2. Send it on WhatsApp with a short, friendly note as soon as each job is done.
  3. Turn it into a QR code for receipts, invoices and a card at the counter.
  4. Ask every customer, not only those you expect to be pleased. Asking only happy customers is against Google's policies.

Never buy reviews, offer discounts or gifts for them, or ask staff and relatives to write them. Google removes reviews it believes are fake and can take action against the profiles involved.

How to reply

Reply to every review within a day or two, and thank happy customers by name. With negative reviews, stay calm, because future customers are reading too: acknowledge the problem without arguing, offer a number to call or WhatsApp, and never share personal details. Google won't remove a review just because it's negative, but you can report one that breaks its policies, such as spam.

Step 7: Keep your name, address and phone the same everywhere

Google builds its picture of your business partly from information across the web, including directories. If your name, address and phone number (often called NAP) differ from site to site, they're harder to trust.

  • Write each detail one way and copy it everywhere. For customers abroad, use the international phone format: +263, then the number without its first 0.
  • Check your website, Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp Business profile, LinkedIn, Zimbabwean business directories and industry association listings.
  • Claim listings on other map services where you can, such as Bing Places.
  • Keep a spreadsheet of every listing, so changes reach all of them quickly.

Step 8: Give each area its own page on your website

If you serve several towns or suburbs, create a page for each, but only where you have something specific to say: what you offer there, how quickly you can arrive, the suburbs you cover, any call-out or delivery charges, photos of real jobs nearby and how to reach you. Don't copy one page and change only the town name. Google's spam policies warn against near-identical "doorway" pages like these.

Link each branch profile to its own page, and add LocalBusiness structured data so search engines can read your details clearly. If your current site makes this hard, our website development team can help.

Step 9: Track calls, direction requests and enquiries

Your profile's Performance section shows how many people found you, the searches they used, and how many called, asked for directions or clicked through to your website. Compare month to month, and remember these figures count taps, not sales.

  • Add tracking tags to your profile's website link, for example ?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp, so Google Analytics shows those visits separately.
  • Track clicks on your phone and WhatsApp buttons as events in Google Analytics.
  • Ask every new customer how they found you, and write it down.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to appear on Google Maps?

Once your profile is verified, it can start appearing for searches of your business name fairly quickly. Ranking for wider searches, such as "electrician in Harare", usually takes steady work over several months and depends on how many competitors you have and how active they are. Nobody can honestly promise you a date or a position.

Can I get on Google Maps if I work from home or don't have a shop?

Yes, as long as you meet customers in person, for example at their homes, sites or offices. Hide your address and set a service area instead. Businesses that operate only online aren't eligible for a Google Business Profile.

Can my staff or family leave reviews for the business?

No. Google's policies don't allow reviews from people with a conflict of interest, such as owners and staff, or reviews given in exchange for discounts or gifts. Reviews from relatives aren't honest feedback either. Ask real customers after real jobs, every time, and let your reviews build up honestly.
