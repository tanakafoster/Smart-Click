Marketing to Zimbabweans abroad: a practical guide for local businesses

How Zimbabwean businesses can sell to Zimbabweans abroad: what they buy, building trust, clear payment, WhatsApp sales, ad targeting and timing.

7 November 2026 · 7 min read · By the Smart Click Media team

Many Zimbabweans living in South Africa, the UK, the US, Australia and elsewhere regularly pay for things at home: groceries for parents, school fees, stands, building materials, solar systems and funeral cover. Given the choice, plenty would rather pay a reliable business directly than send cash and hope it's spent as planned. This guide shows how a Zimbabwean business can earn their trust and turn it into steady, repeat orders.

Who you're really selling to

Your typical customer is a working adult abroad supporting parents, children in school or the wider family. Every sale involves two people: the buyer, who pays from abroad and wants proof the money did what it was meant to, and the recipient, who receives the goods and reports back. Both need a good experience. Some buyers have been away for years, so don't assume they know current prices or how things work locally.

What they buy for family back home

Each purchase comes with its own worry. Answer it in your marketing before the buyer has to ask.

What they buyWhat worries themWhat reassures them
Groceries and hampersWill it all arrive, fresh and complete?An itemised list, a delivery photo and a message from the recipient
School feesHas the school really been paid?Paying the school directly, and sending the school's own receipt
Stands and housesIs the land genuine, and is it the seller's to sell?Documents explained plainly, a video site tour and advice to use their own lawyer
Building materialsWill the full order reach the site?A delivery note matching the invoice, and photos of the materials on site
Funeral coverWhat's covered, and will it pay out quickly?Clear policy wording, who underwrites it and a simple claims process
Solar systemsWill it be installed properly, and who fixes it later?An itemised quote, installation photos, a written warranty and an after-sales contact

Trust is the main barrier, so build it on purpose

Buyers abroad can't walk into your shop, and many have heard stories of money sent home that went missing. Expect to be checked, and make checking easy.

  • Show you're real: your registered company name, a physical address in Zimbabwe, a phone that's answered, and a website and email on your own domain.
  • Show real faces: a short video of the owner and team, and a live video call so buyers can see the stock, warehouse or stand before they pay.
  • Prove every delivery with a photo or short video of the handover, taken with the recipient's permission.
  • Publish your policies on refunds, substitutions, delays and cancellations.
  • Protect your payment details: use only accounts in the business's name, publish them on your website, and say you'll never change them by message alone.
  • Let people start small. A hamper is an easier first yes than a stand deposit.

Many buyers will also check your Google reviews, and our local SEO service can help you build genuine ones.

Make payment and delivery impossible to misunderstand

Confusion about money is where trust breaks fastest. Before anyone pays, they should know:

  • the full price in US dollars, including delivery and fees, with nothing added later
  • which payment methods you accept from abroad, and how long each takes to clear
  • when delivery happens, and which areas you cover
  • what happens if an item is out of stock
  • what the recipient must do, such as be home or sign for the goods
  • what proof they'll get: an invoice, a receipt and delivery confirmation

Put all of this on one "How it works" page and send the link in your first WhatsApp reply, or let an e-commerce store with international payments and set delivery zones answer these questions for you.

WhatsApp is your shop counter

For many diaspora buyers, WhatsApp is where questions get asked and orders placed, so run it like a staffed counter.

  • Use the WhatsApp Business app, with your address, hours and website in your profile.
  • Build a catalogue with photos and prices, so buyers can browse without waiting.
  • Save quick replies for common questions, set an away message that says when you'll reply, and use labels to track orders: enquiry, quote sent, paid, delivered.
  • Use one official number on your website, ads, Google profile, receipts and vehicles.
  • Answer when buyers are free: a US buyer's evening falls in your night, and an Australian buyer's evening is your late morning or early afternoon.

Only send promotions to people who've opted in, and make opting out easy. Busier teams can move to the WhatsApp Business Platform, usually through a provider, to automate messages. Our email and WhatsApp marketing team can set up either.

Target by country on Meta and Google

Run a separate campaign for each country. Pay days, time zones and currencies differ, and separate campaigns let you set the budget and see the results for each market.

Meta: Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp

  • Set the location to the country, or to the cities where your customers live.
  • Use ads that open a WhatsApp chat.
  • Build audiences from website visitors, people who engaged with your page and customers who agreed to hear from you, and let Meta find similar people in each country.
  • Don't rely on interest settings to find Zimbabweans. They change, and your own audiences plus ads that clearly mention Zimbabwe are more dependable.

Google

  • Target the country your buyers live in, choosing people in or regularly in that location.
  • Use keywords that name Zimbabwe or a town, such as "groceries delivered to Harare" or "stands for sale in Harare".
  • Schedule ads for the buyer's hours, not Harare's.
  • Send each country's clicks to a landing page written for that country.

Tag links by country so you can see which market brings paying customers, not just clicks. Our paid ads team runs diaspora campaigns on both platforms.

Time it around pay days, school terms and holidays

Ask your customers when they're paid. As a starting point:

MarketTime compared with HararePay patterns to check
South AfricaSame timeMonthly pay is common, often towards the end of the month
United Kingdom1 or 2 hours behind, depending on the seasonMonthly pay is common
United States6 to 10 hours behind on the mainlandMany people are paid every two weeks
Australia6 to 9 hours ahead, depending on city and seasonFortnightly pay is common
  • School terms: most schools run three terms, starting around January, May and September. Promote fee payments a few weeks before each term, and check each year's official dates.
  • The festive season: start hamper campaigns in early November and publish a firm cut-off date for Christmas delivery.
  • Mother's Day: the UK marks it in March or early April, but South Africa, the US and Australia mark it in May, so plan each country separately.

Content and testimonials: real ones only

Useful content answers questions buyers are too polite to ask: a "how it works" video, delivery handovers filmed with permission, dated building progress updates, stand walk-arounds and price updates on WhatsApp Status.

Real testimonials are some of the most convincing content you can have. Never invent, borrow or "improve" them: fakes are dishonest, can break advertising and consumer rules, and one exposed fake can undo years of goodwill. Collect real ones with every order instead:

  1. A few days after delivery, ask the buyer how it went.
  2. If they're happy, ask for a sentence, a voice note or a short video.
  3. Get written permission to use their words, first name, country and photo, and keep a record.
  4. Ask the recipient too: a mother in Gweru confirming her hamper arrived means a lot to the next buyer.

Never change a testimonial's meaning, never use stock photos as "customers", and hide phone numbers in screenshots.

Start with one product, one country and one well-run WhatsApp number, then grow from there. If you'd like help planning it, contact us to book a free strategy session.

Frequently asked questions

Which country should we target first?

Start where you already have customers or enquiries. If you have none yet, ask your local customers where their relatives abroad live. One well-run campaign in one country usually teaches you more than a small budget spread thinly across four.

Should we price in US dollars or in the buyer's currency?

US dollars are usually simplest, because many prices in Zimbabwe are already set in US dollars. If you also accept pounds, rand or other currencies, explain how the exchange rate is set and when it's fixed, so the buyer isn't surprised by the final amount.

How do we get testimonials if we're just starting out?

Ask your first customers directly after a successful delivery, and get their permission in writing before you use anything. Until you have some, show other proof instead: your team, your premises, delivery photos and clear policies. Never invent a testimonial or borrow one from another business.
